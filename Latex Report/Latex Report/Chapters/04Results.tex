\chapter{Feasibility Discussion} \label{ch:results}
\vspace{-1cm}
% ----------------------------------------------------
With the final design shown in \autoref{ch:design}, it can be seen that it is possible to achieve sufficient containment capabilities for the purpose of containing cell cultures intended for BSL-3 Laboratories.

The use of an o-ring to seal the lid to the base would provide adequate containment as this is a common practice used in ingress protection(IP) techniques. The IP rating \cite{IngressProtectionIP} is a rating that is used to determine the protection from water and dust ingress for a product. The IP rating that best applies to this design is that illustrated by the IP68 requirements, which state the a device should be capable of handling full submersion in liquids without any ingress. This would provide sufficient containment for contagious cell cultures and thus would be suitable for a BSL-3 laboratory. 

IP68 containers are commonly made with the use of o-rings, as they provide complete seals whilst under pressure, and whilst still allowing easy access to the container. One potential issue is that the bio-containment chamber needs to be compatible with typical sterilisation techniques, including the autoclave and chemical disinfectants. Most o-rings are not capable of surviving the harsh conditions present in an autoclave, and so consideration must be taken to ensure that the  o-ring material is compatible with these conditions. One such material that is commonly used in medical laboratories is Silicone, which according to the Parker handbook \cite[73]{ORingHandbook}, is resistant to temperatures up to 210\degree C.

The use of a silicon based gasket present on the bottom of the base, which is used to seal the multi-well plate frame to the chamber, in conjunction with the o-ring present on the lid, would ensure that interior of the chamber is sufficiently sealed to prevent contamination. 

Material choice for the frame needs to be taken into consideration, since the chamber needs to withstand the serialisation and disinfection standards mentioned above. High temperature and pressure steam used in autoclaves promotes corrosion in certain metals, and so a corrosion resistant metal needs to be used to avoid damage. Additionally, the harsh chemicals used by disinfectants, such as bleach which acts as an oxidiser and so can easily corrode and deteriorate materials.

The use of screws to secure the lid and multi-well plate does introduce some difficulty with using the chamber, however this mechanism ensures that once the lid is properly fastened to the base, the container will remained sealed and will not potentially open during the experiments.

Overall this conceptual design shows strong evidence to suggest that a fully sealed bio-containment chamber can be made using available materials and manufacturing techniques and allow for the safe containment of potentially hazardous cell cultures that are handled in a BSL-3 laboratory. The containment chamber can be improved upon to allow for thermal control, this would be the first step into designing an incubation chamber that could be used in lab experiments.
